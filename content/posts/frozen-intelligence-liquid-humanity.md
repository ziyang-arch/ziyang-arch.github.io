Today’s world is moving at an unprecedented velocity. Technological breakthroughs are birthing new social landscapes daily, yet this historical process—while enriching our sensory experiences—leaves many feeling adrift.

The abundance of material wealth has brought about a vacuum of values. Human beings cannot tolerate a moral void; this is precisely why we need a new morality to anchor ourselves in the age of Artificial Intelligence.

## The Shifting Landscape: What Has Changed?

In the AI era, we are witnessing the first "reification" (物化) of intelligence in human history.

**The Reification of Intelligence:** Historically, intelligence was a liquid asset, residing within living human minds. The ruling class (Capital) had to employ, persuade, or collaborate with people to access this intelligence.

**AI as "Frozen Intelligence":** Large Language Models (LLMs) are essentially the compression of millions of human hours and global data into static weight files. Once Capital acquires compute (GPUs) and these weights, it no longer depends on the input of "average" or even "mid-level" human intellect.

**The Closed Loop of Power:** If Capital now owns the machines that generate intelligence, the "Prime Movers" described by Ayn Rand no longer require a social contract. This is the ultimate subversion of the democratic cornerstone: the idea that everyone has an inherent value because they are needed by society.

## Anchoring Personal Value

The value of morality lies in its ability to affirm the individual, providing a spiritual anchor. From the scholars of the Spring and Autumn period in China to the philosophers of Ancient Greece, moral systems have always been "version-controlled" to fit their era. However, when Machiavelli exposed the cold logic of power and Nihilism shattered the "God" illusion, we were forced to look the other way.

### Kant’s Solution: Dignity and Ends

Immanuel Kant argued that morality is grounded in the autonomy of reason. He proposed the "Formula of Humanity":

> "Handle so, dass du die Menschheit sowohl in deiner Person, als in der Person eines jeden anderen jederzeit zugleich als Zweck, niemals bloß als Mittel brauchest."
> —— (Act in such a way that you treat humanity... always at the same time as an end, never merely as a means.)

In the AI age, this formula is our final defensive line. If a person's worth is defined solely by their productivity, then in the face of AI, humans become mere "tools with a price tag" rather than "ends with dignity."

### Nietzsche’s Challenge: The Last Man

Nietzsche urged us to command ourselves. Without a new moral anchor, the extreme convenience provided by AI may push us toward what Nietzsche called the "Last Man" (Der letzte Mensch):

> "One still works, for work is a form of entertainment. But one is careful lest the entertainment be too fiery."
> —— Thus Spoke Zarathustra

The risk of the AI era is that when "effort" loses its uniqueness, humanity may lose the "Will to Power"—the drive for self-overcoming—and instead stagnate in a mediocre comfort zone fed by algorithms.

## AI Capital’s Constraints: Rand’s Mirage vs. Reality

The current narrative of the AI industry is, in many ways, a violent validation of Ayn Rand’s Objectivism.

### AI as the "Validation" of Randian Ideals

In Rand’s journals, the world is moved by a tiny elite of "Prime Movers," while the rest are merely "parasites":

> "The world is moved by the 'Prime Movers'—the men of creative ability. The rest of mankind are merely parasites." (1945 Journal)

Today’s AI elite seem to be recreating this narrative. By monopolizing compute and data, they are attempting to build a digital "Galt’s Gulch"—a sanctuary of technology that is decoupled from the masses.

### The Equation of Power: $Capital \approx Intelligence$

This is the most fundamental shift in power.

* **Static Intelligence:** Intelligence has become a commodity that can be rented by the hour and replicated infinitely by Capital.
* **The Death of Resistance:** Individual manual productivity has zero leverage against a GPU cluster.

Capital is no longer a follower of intelligence; Capital is the owner of intelligence.

## Redistribution and Moral Practice: Dismantling Digital Feudalism

If AI elites hold the power to price resources and collect "rent" on human knowledge, Kantian "dignity" becomes a hollow promise.

### Rawls’s "Veil of Ignorance"

We must extend Kantian logic into the realm of redistribution. John Rawls proposed that the rules of justice should be decided behind a "Veil of Ignorance."

Rawls’s "Difference Principle" states that social and economic inequalities are only just if they result in compensating benefits for everyone, and in particular for the "least advantaged" members of society.

## Conclusion: What Morality Do We Need?

If Kant provided us with the "Base Protocol" of human dignity, then John Rawls provided the "Code Audit" for AI-era distribution.

When we see the equation $Capital \approx Intelligence$ tearing society into "Compute Landlords" and "Digital Serfs," the Randian hero narrative is revealed as morally bankrupt. True elites should not be escapees in a "Galt’s Gulch"; they must be the executors of a social contract—recognizing that their intellectual leverage is a gift from the collective civilization and using it to drive a production revolution that benefits the very bottom of society.
